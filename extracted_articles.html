A history of tapioca pearls

From a humble dessert topping in Taiwan to a global phenomenon, the journey of the tapioca pearl is a fascinating tale of culinary innovation. Derived from the starch of the cassava root, tapioca was originally used in traditional shaved ice desserts before an inventive tea shop owner decided to drop a few chewy balls into a glass of cold milk tea in the late 1980s. The contrasting textures of the smooth, sweet tea and the bouncy, gummy-like pearls created an instant sensation. Today, "boba" has evolved into an entire subculture, with countless variations of flavors, colors, and consistencies. We dive deep into the archives to trace how this simple ingredient transformed the modern beverage industry and became a cultural icon across the world.
